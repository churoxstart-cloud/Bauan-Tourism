Mission

To attain sustainability through the provision of basic social services, employment, livelihood, and adequate infrastructure, and support facilities while promoting economic growth and an ecologically-balanced environment through responsive and holistic development approaches.

Vision

Bauan 2040: a haven of sustainable Industry, Agri-Industry, Commerce and Trade, and Tourism, mobilized with God-centered and competitive individuals; living in a green, adaptable, and resilient environment and economy, shaped through efficient and effective governance.

About The Town

A Rich Heritage by the Sea

The history of Bauan is a rich narrative of resilience, faith, and cultural heritage, beginning around 1590 when Augustinian priests founded a small settlement on the slopes of Mount Macolod near the shores of Taal Lake...

Bauan’s spiritual and cultural identity deeply shaped its character, anchored by the discovery of a sacred wooden cross around 1595 that the townspeople believed protected them from natural disasters...

Got Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get to Bauan from Manila via public commute?
You can ride a bus from terminals in Cubao or Buendia headed straight to the Batangas City Grand Terminal. From the grand terminal, step out to catch a local jeepney bound directly for Bauan town proper.
Are there ATM services available in Bauan?
Yes, multiple major commercial banks and ATMs are stationed around the municipal center. However, it is always recommended to bring sufficient cash if you are visiting remote beach or diving resorts.
Do I need pre-booking for scuba diving packages?
While walk-ins are occasionally accommodated, pre-booking with certified local dive operators is strongly advised especially during weekends and peak dry season months to secure gear and instructors.
When is the best time to visit Bauan?
The dry season, roughly November to May, has the calmest seas and clearest water for swimming and diving. March to May is the warmest and busiest, so book early. From June to October, rain and the habagat (southwest monsoon) can make the sea rough; check the weather and PAGASA advisories before you go.
How do I get to Bauan by private car?
From Metro Manila, take SLEX, continue to the STAR Tollway, and exit toward Batangas City, then follow the national road to Bauan. Without heavy traffic the drive is about 2 to 3 hours. Most beach and dive resorts are in coastal barangays such as Sampaguita, San Pablo and La Liga Locloc, so use the Travel Guide map for exact directions.
How do I get around once I'm in Bauan?
Jeepneys run along the main roads, and tricycles can take you from the town proper or the highway to resorts in the coastal barangays. Agree on the fare before the ride, especially for longer trips to beach areas. If you have a lot of gear (for example, dive equipment), a private car or a resort pick-up is easier.
Can I book a resort through this website?
Not yet. This site is run by the Municipal Tourism Office to help you find places; it does not take bookings or payments. Open a resort's page (tap Explore) to see its contact number, then reserve and confirm rates directly with the resort.
Are there entrance or environmental fees?
Resorts charge their own entrance or day-use fees, and some dive sites and marine sanctuaries collect a separate fee used for their protection. Amounts can change, so confirm with the resort, your dive operator, or the MTO before you go. Each place's page shows the fee if the MTO has posted it.
What should I bring for a beach or dive trip?
Bring reef-safe sunscreen, a hat, drinking water, a reusable bottle, a dry bag, water shoes, cash for small stores and tricycles, and a valid ID. Divers should bring their certification card and logbook. Please take your trash home with you: Bauan's reefs and beaches depend on it.
What are the rules in the marine sanctuaries?
Do not touch, stand on, or collect corals, shells or marine life, and do not feed the fish. Fishing and spearfishing are not allowed inside sanctuaries. Follow your dive guide, keep good buoyancy, and use only mooring buoys instead of dropping anchor on the reef.
What local food should I try?
Try Batangas lomi (thick noodle soup), bulalo (beef shank soup), goto, tapang Taal, and a cup of strong kapeng barako. Fresh seafood is easy to find near the coast, and the Bauan public market is a good stop for pasalubong.
Is Bauan good for families with kids?
Yes. Many resorts have pools and shallow areas, and short trips such as a beach day, a church visit and a food stop work well with kids. Some shorelines are rocky or drop off quickly, so bring water shoes, keep children supervised, and ask the resort where it is safe to swim.
Who do I contact in an emergency or for tourist assistance?
For emergencies, call 911. For tourist information, lost items, or complaints about a tourism establishment, contact the Municipal Tourism Office of Bauan through the Contact page. You can also ask the front desk of your resort for the nearest police station or hospital.
